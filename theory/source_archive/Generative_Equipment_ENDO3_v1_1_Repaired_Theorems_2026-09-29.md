# 0. Executive repair

The stress test found that the grammar-repair idea is sound, but five statements needed correction:

1. nonempty repair category with no initial object does **not** imply multiple repair types;
2. pointed repair in an \(\infty\)-category must include a coherent homotopy \(bu\simeq ra\);
3. free/unconstrained repairs do not automatically satisfy a preservation contract;
4. the meet of all repair doctrines need not itself be a repair;
5. one-step mutation is generally a monotone inflationary **preclosure**, not an idempotent closure operator.

A sixth issue remains structural: the defect compiler is not uniquely forced by the previous compiler axioms.  v1.1 repairs this as far as possible by introducing a **law-relative canonical occurrence compiler**.

The repaired theory is best described as

\[
\boxed{\text{relative diagnostic-complete grammar-repair theory}}
\]

and is constructively complete in reflective/accessibly generated repair sectors.

# 1. Homotopy-coherent pointed repairs

Let \(\mathbf{Gram}\) be an \(\infty\)-category with pushouts.  Fix a walking defect

\[
A\xrightarrow{u}B,\qquad A\xrightarrow{a}G.
\]

A pointed repair with target \(X\) consists of

\[
r:G\to X,\qquad b:B\to X,
\]

and a specified 2-simplex / homotopy

\[
\eta:bu\simeq ra.
\]

Morphisms of repairs are maps of targets compatible with all this data up to the prescribed coherent homotopies.

Define

\[
P:=G\amalg_A B.
\]

## Theorem 1.1 — Pointed Repair Slice Theorem

There is a canonical equivalence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Rep}^{\mathrm{pt}}(u,a)
\simeq
\mathbf{Gram}_{P/}.
}
\]

### Proof

For each target \(X\), the pushout universal property gives

\[
\operatorname{Map}(P,X)
\simeq
\operatorname{Map}(G,X)
\times_{\operatorname{Map}(A,X)}
\operatorname{Map}(B,X).
\]

A point of the homotopy pullback is precisely \((r,b,\eta)\).  Naturality in \(X\) identifies the Grothendieck construction of pointed repairs with the undercategory \(\mathbf{Gram}_{P/}\). \(\square\)

## Corollary 1.2 — Unconstrained universal repair

The object

\[
\operatorname{id}_P:P\to P
\]

is initial in \(\operatorname{Rep}^{\mathrm{pt}}(u,a)\).

Thus the walking pushout theorem remains valid, but only after the homotopy-coherent repair definition is used.

# 2. Preservation contracts and constrained repair

Let

\[
\mathcal R_0:=\mathbf{Gram}_{P/}
\]

be the unconstrained pointed repair category.  A preservation contract \(K\) determines a full subcategory

\[
\mathcal R_K\subseteq \mathcal R_0.
\]

Examples of preservation clauses include full faithfulness of the old semantics, preservation of marked relations, preservation of designated monoidal structure, nonvanishing of specified old objects, or observation-reflection requirements.

## Theorem 2.1 — Reflective Constrained Repair Theorem

Assume \(\mathcal R_K\) is reflective in \(\mathcal R_0\):

\[
L:\mathcal R_0\rightleftarrows \mathcal R_K:i,
\qquad L\dashv i.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
L(\operatorname{id}_P)
}
\]

is initial in \(\mathcal R_K\).

### Proof

For any \(X\in\mathcal R_K\),

\[
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal R_K}
(L\operatorname{id}_P,X)
\simeq
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal R_0}
(\operatorname{id}_P,iX).
\]

The right-hand side is contractible because \(\operatorname{id}_P\) is initial in \(\mathcal R_0\).  Hence \(L\operatorname{id}_P\) is initial in \(\mathcal R_K\). \(\square\)

## Corollary 2.2 — Accessible constrained repair

If \(\mathbf{Gram}\) is presentable, then \(\mathcal R_0=\mathbf{Gram}_{P/}\) is presentable.  If the \(K\)-admissible repairs are the local objects for an accessible localization of this slice (for example one generated by a small family of morphisms), then a universal constrained repair exists and is obtained by reflecting \(\operatorname{id}_P\).

### Boundary

Algebraic small-object completion, localization, and free weighted cocompletion remain standard **unconstrained/free repair engines**.  Preservation of \(K\) must be proved separately, e.g. by Theorem 2.1.

# 3. Joint rather than pointwise repair

If several intrinsic defects are active simultaneously, individual universal repairs need not be jointly compatible with a preservation contract.

Let

\[
\{A_i\xrightarrow{u_i}B_i,\;A_i\xrightarrow{a_i}G\}_{i\in I}
\]

be a small family of current pointed defect occurrences.  Form

\[
P_I
:=
G\amalg_{\coprod_i A_i}\coprod_i B_i.
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — Batch Repair Slice Theorem

The \(\infty\)-category of simultaneous pointed repairs of all listed occurrences is canonically

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf{Gram}_{P_I/}.
}
\]

In particular, \(\operatorname{id}_{P_I}\) is the unconstrained universal simultaneous repair.

### Proof

Apply the pushout mapping-space formula to the coproduct family. \(\square\)

## Corollary 3.2 — Constrained batch repair

If the full subcategory of simultaneous \(K\)-admissible repairs is reflective in \(\mathbf{Gram}_{P_I/}\), then the reflection of \(\operatorname{id}_{P_I}\) is the universal joint constrained repair.

### Principle

One-step grammar mutation must be computed from the **joint repair category** of all currently active defects.  Per-defect initial repairs may be composed only after an interchange/compatibility theorem has been proved.

# 4. Repair Profile replaces the old Repair Trichotomy interpretation

Let

\[
\mathcal R:=\operatorname{Rep}_K(\Sigma,\delta)
\]

be the full repair \(\infty\)-category.  Define

\[
\mathfrak R:=\mathcal R^{\simeq}
\]

and the universal locus

\[
\mathfrak U:=\bigl(\mathcal R^{\mathrm{init}}\bigr)^{\simeq}.
\]

The space \(\mathfrak U\) is either empty or contractible.

## Definition 4.1 — Repair Profile

The repair profile is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Prof}_{\mathrm{rep}}(\Sigma,\delta;K)
=
(\mathcal R,\mathfrak U,\pi_0\mathfrak R,\{\Omega_r\mathfrak R\}_{r\in\mathfrak R}).
}
\]

It records feasibility, universality, repair types, isotropy, and higher symmetry.

## Theorem 4.2 — Repair Profile Classification

Exactly one of the following coarse cases holds:

1. **Infeasible:** \(\mathcal R=\varnothing\).
2. **Universal locus present:** \(\mathfrak U\neq\varnothing\).  A universal repair exists and is unique up to contractible choice.
3. **Feasible but nonuniversal:** \(\mathcal R\neq\varnothing\) and \(\mathfrak U=\varnothing\).

Case 3 must then be refined by the core geometry:

- \(|\pi_0\mathfrak R|>1\): genuine repair-type branching;
- \(\mathfrak R\) connected but noncontractible: unique repair type with isotropy/higher symmetry;
- \(\mathfrak R\simeq *\) but \(\mathfrak U=\varnothing\): unique rigid equivalence type but no universal mapping property.

### Remark

An initial object may coexist with many non-initial repair types.  Therefore “universal repair exists” and “repair moduli branches” are independent diagnostics rather than mutually exclusive semantic labels.

# 5. Counterexamples forcing the Repair Profile

## Example 5.1 — Unique type with isotropy

Let \(k\) be a field with \(k^\times\neq1\), and let \(\mathcal R\) be the groupoid of one-dimensional \(k\)-vector spaces and isomorphisms.

Then

\[
\mathcal R\simeq Bk^\times,
\qquad
\pi_0\mathcal R=*,
\]

so there is only one repair equivalence type, but no initial object because every object has nontrivial automorphisms.

## Example 5.2 — Contractible core without initiality

Let \(\mathcal C\) have one object \(*\) and endomorphism monoid

\[
\{1,e\},\qquad e^2=e,
\]

with \(e\neq1\).

The maximal groupoid \(\mathcal C^{\simeq}\) is a point, but \(*\) is not initial because \(\operatorname{Hom}(*,*)\) has two elements.

Thus even \(\mathfrak R\simeq *\) does not imply a universal repair.

# 6. Preservation relaxation frontiers remain valid

For a finite contract

\[
K=\{k_1,\ldots,k_m\},
\]

let a subcontract \(J\subseteq K\) be feasible when \(\operatorname{Rep}_J\neq\varnothing\).

Feasibility is downward closed.  The maximal feasible subcontracts form the relaxation frontier.

This result survives unchanged.

The frontier diagnoses *which preservation losses are unavoidable*, but does not choose among incomparable frontier points.

# 7. Mandatory doctrine kernel replaces “forced repair core”

Suppose repair doctrines are represented by elements of a complete lattice \(L\), and let

\[
\mathcal F\subseteq L
\]

be the nonempty set of feasible repair doctrines.

Define

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Mand}(\mathcal F)
:=
\bigwedge_{\rho\in\mathcal F}\rho.
}
\]

This is the **mandatory doctrine kernel**: the doctrine content common to every feasible repair.

## Theorem 7.1 — Kernel versus repair

\(\operatorname{Mand}(\mathcal F)\) is a repair iff

\[
\operatorname{Mand}(\mathcal F)\in\mathcal F.
\]

In particular, if \(\mathcal F\) is closed under the relevant meet, then the mandatory kernel is the least feasible repair in the doctrine order.

### Proof

The meet is, by definition, the greatest lower bound of all feasible repairs.  It becomes a feasible repair exactly when it belongs to \(\mathcal F\).  In that case it is below every feasible repair and hence is least. \(\square\)

### Boundary

Categorical initiality and leastness in a doctrine poset are different notions.  They coincide only after proving that repair morphisms are faithfully represented by the doctrine order.

# 8. Law-relative canonical defect compiler

The old compiler axioms do not determine a unique compiler: the empty compiler satisfies naturality, smallness, saturation invariance and provenance vacuously.

The repair is to separate:

- the **law signature**, which is declared structural input;
- the **occurrence compiler**, which can then be canonical.

Let a small law signature be

\[
\mathbb L
=
\{(u_\lambda:A_\lambda\to B_\lambda,\tau_\lambda,K_\lambda)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},
\]

where \(\tau_\lambda\) is a homotopy-invariant satisfaction predicate on the saturated filler space (nonempty, contractible, equivalence, etc.).

For a grammar \(G\), let

\[
\operatorname{Occ}_\lambda(G)=\operatorname{Map}(A_\lambda,G).
\]

Let \(\operatorname{Def}_\lambda(G)\subseteq\operatorname{Occ}_\lambda(G)\) consist of the saturated occurrences whose filler spaces fail \(\tau_\lambda\).

Define

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr C_{\mathbb L}(G)
=
\coprod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
\operatorname{Def}_\lambda(G).
}
\]

## Theorem 8.1 — Canonical Occurrence Compiler

Assume the law signature transports under the declared grammar equivalences and each \(\tau_\lambda\) is homotopy invariant.  Then \(\mathscr C_{\mathbb L}\) is canonical relative to \(\mathbb L\):

1. equivalent grammar states have equivalent compiled defect spaces;
2. every defect occurrence of a declared law is compiled—there is no per-occurrence selection;
3. if \(\Lambda\) is small and \(\mathbf{Gram}\) locally small, the compiled family is small in the chosen universe;
4. provenance is inherited from \((\lambda,a)\).

### Proof

Equivalences induce equivalences of mapping spaces and therefore preserve the homotopy-invariant satisfaction predicates.  Taking the coproduct over the fixed small law signature gives the result. \(\square\)

### Residual boundary

The law signature \(\mathbb L\) itself is not generated by this theorem.  Thus v1.1 solves **occurrence selection**, not absolute law-signature genesis.

# 9. Compiler Non-Uniqueness No-Go

## Theorem 9.1

Naturality, saturation invariance, smallness, provenance, and anti-target-peeking alone cannot characterize a unique nontrivial defect compiler.

### Proof

The identically empty compiler satisfies naturality, saturation invariance and smallness, with provenance vacuous; it also cannot peek at a target because it emits nothing.  Whenever a nonempty admissible compiler exists on some state, the two compilers differ. \(\square\)

Therefore absolute compiler genesis requires an additional nondegeneracy/completeness principle or a declared law signature.

# 10. Preclosure and genuine doctrine closure

Let \(L\) be a set-sized complete lattice and

\[
T:L\to L
\]

be monotone and inflationary:

\[
x\le T(x).
\]

Do **not** call \(T\) a closure operator unless \(T^2=T\).

Define transfinite iteration from \(x\):

\[
x_0=x,
\qquad
x_{\alpha+1}=T(x_\alpha),
\qquad
x_\lambda=\bigvee_{\beta<\lambda}x_\beta.
\]

Because \(L\) is set-sized, the increasing chain stabilizes at some ordinal.  Let

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{cl}_T(x)
}
\]

be the first stable value.

## Theorem 10.1 — Fixed-Point Closure Theorem

\(\operatorname{cl}_T\) is a genuine closure operator:

\[
x\le\operatorname{cl}_T(x),
\]

\[
x\le y
\Rightarrow
\operatorname{cl}_T(x)\le\operatorname{cl}_T(y),
\]

\[
\operatorname{cl}_T(\operatorname{cl}_T(x))
=
\operatorname{cl}_T(x).
\]

Moreover \(\operatorname{cl}_T(x)\) is the least \(T\)-fixed point above \(x\).

### Proof

Transfinite induction shows every \(T\)-fixed point above \(x\) dominates the entire iteration.  Set-sizedness forces stabilization.  The stabilized point is fixed and is therefore least.  Extensivity and monotonicity follow from the construction; idempotence follows because a fixed point is already stable. \(\square\)

The grammar closure ordinal remains relative to \(T\).

# 11. Revised one-step mutation

Given a law signature \(\mathbb L\):

1. compile **all** saturated active defect occurrences using \(\mathscr C_{\mathbb L}\);
2. form the joint repair category for the entire current batch;
3. impose the combined preservation contract;
4. compute the Repair Profile;
5. if the admissible batch-repair category is reflective, take the reflected initial repair;
6. if feasible but nonuniversal, retain the full repair moduli and its mandatory doctrine kernel;
7. if infeasible, compute the relaxation frontier when the contract is finite.

Only a sector in which the **joint** admissible repair category has an initial object is deterministic.

# 12. Revised Relative Grammar-Genesis Theorem

## Theorem 12.1 — Diagnostic completeness

Fix:

1. a presentable grammar universe or a locally presentable 1-categorical analogue;
2. a small law signature \(\mathbb L\);
3. the canonical occurrence compiler \(\mathscr C_{\mathbb L}\);
4. explicit preservation contracts;
5. saturation/gauge data;
6. observation and provenance doctrines.

Then each current batch of intrinsic defects determines a canonical joint repair category and hence a canonical Repair Profile.

Thus the theory always distinguishes:

- infeasible repair;
- universal-repair locus;
- genuine type branching;
- isotropy/higher-symmetry nonuniqueness;
- rigid nonuniversal repair types;
- preservation-relaxation frontiers (for finite contracts).

This is **relative diagnostic completeness**.

## Theorem 12.2 — Constructive completeness in reflective sectors

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 12.1, assume additionally that the joint \(K\)-admissible repair category is a reflective subcategory of the unconstrained repair slice.

Then a universal joint constrained repair exists and is obtained by reflecting the unconstrained initial repair.

If each iteration step again satisfies this hypothesis and induces a monotone inflationary map on the doctrine lattice, the fixed-point closure \(\operatorname{cl}_T\) gives the least stable deterministic doctrine above the initial one.
